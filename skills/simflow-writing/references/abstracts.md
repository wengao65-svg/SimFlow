# Abstracts

Make the scientific contribution identifiable without turning the abstract into
a miniature Methods section or validation report. Respect the requested length
and supplied journal format; there is no required sentence count.

## Select the Content

Identify the question or unmet capability, the specific finding or method, its
most informative evidence, and the consequence supported by that evidence.
These are selection questions, not functions that each need a separate sentence.
Background can be brief or implicit for a specialized audience. A theoretical
contribution need not include a benchmark number; a physical finding need not
advertise model accuracy before stating the phenomenon.

Lead with the material, phenomenon, unresolved relation, or methodological
problem that actually motivates the work. Do not default to "DFT is expensive"
or a field-importance preamble. Name the computational approach only to the
extent needed to understand the contribution.

For physical work, prioritize the observed behavior and what it implies. For
methods, identify the new ingredient and demonstrated capability. For resources,
state what coverage or comparison becomes possible. For reliability work,
identify the error source and what can now be inferred or estimated differently.
These emphases can coexist without becoming a list of equal stories.

## Calibrate the Answer

Use the strongest relevant quantitative contrast when available, rather than a
catalog of error metrics, trajectory lengths, and dataset sizes. Name the
comparison and scope behind accuracy, efficiency, or transfer claims. Avoid
"excellent", "universal", and "unprecedented" without support.

Do not use a stock "originates from mechanism Y rather than Z" sentence unless
the evidence actually distinguishes those explanations. "Consistent with" or a
direct description of the observed relation may be the correct main claim.
Retain a scope qualification if omitting it would misrepresent the result.

End on the finding or a specific implication. A new prediction, design rule,
future-work sentence, or broad impact claim is not compulsory.

## Revision Check

Can a reader identify what was learned without reading a list of completed
checks? Does each included number support that message? Remove repetition and
generic background, not qualifications needed to keep the claim accurate.
Do not add sentences merely to fill a field/bottleneck/evidence/impact template.
