# Introductions

Establish the scientific context and the particular question this work can
address. Organize the argument at the length the problem and audience require;
there is no five-paragraph requirement or compulsory simulation-cost narrative.

## From Context to Question

Explain the material, process, theoretical relation, or capability at issue.
Use prior work to establish what is known and what remains ambiguous. The gap
can be a competing interpretation, missing regime, unresolved quantitative
relation, methodological approximation, or poorly characterized uncertainty.
Do not invent a controversy or declare existing approaches inadequate merely
to justify the current calculation.

Choose references for their argumentative role: observations needing explanation,
candidate mechanisms, relevant theory, prior methods, or reference values. Give
credit to what prior work achieved and specify the boundary of what it answered.
Avoid a chronological catalog and avoid unsupported novelty claims.

Introduce DFT, AIMD, MLPs, or other tools when their capabilities or limitations
matter to this question. Cost matters when the required time, length, or sampling
regime is part of the problem; it is not the default motivation for all papers.
A theory paper may appropriately begin with a formal relation or approximation.

## Position the Contribution

Explain how the present evidence bears on the open question and preview the
principal answer at its demonstrated strength. The closing passage can combine
approach and finding; it need not enumerate methods, follow figure order, or
promise a mechanism and design rule.

For example, distinguishing transient contacts from persistent molecular species
motivates a different study from reducing the sample cost of a transferable
potential. Neither benefits from inheriting the other's introduction sequence.

## Revision Check

Can the reader identify the open question and how this work addresses it?
Are the proposed mechanism and novelty no stronger here than in Results?
Remove textbook background and generic tool promotion, but retain prior findings
needed to understand the question. Do not force every cited paragraph to end
with a gap statement or put the contribution in a fixed paragraph position.
