# Data and Resource Contributions

Use for databases, reference datasets, curated simulation collections, and
reusable computational resources. Establish what scientific comparisons or
questions the resource makes possible, not merely that records passed checks.

## Coverage and Scientific Use

Describe the resource's contents and construction sufficiently to understand
what is represented. Connect demonstrations to a specific use: testing a
relation, comparing methods, exploring underrepresented configurations, or
training and evaluating models. Do not manufacture a mechanism or universal
law to justify a useful resource contribution.

Distinguish dataset size from representativeness. Unequal composition counts,
temperature coverage, selection procedures, and reference-label differences
can confound apparent trends. A restricted comparison, such as a binary subset,
may clarify one relation while narrowing the population to which it applies.

## Evidence and Organization

Specify relevant provenance, versions, units, reference states, exclusions,
duplicates, label consistency, and train/test separation. Describe their
scientific consequences in the main text when they affect interpretation;
place detailed schemas and curation procedures in Methods or supporting material.
Never treat a curated label as proof of phase identity or physical validity.

Coverage, demonstrated utility, interpretability, and availability can be woven
into different orders. There is no mandatory architecture-first or six-figure
plan. State actual access conditions without inventing a public release or DOI.

## Narrative Check

Does the paper show what can be learned using the resource and what comparisons
remain biased or unsupported? Coverage analysis can motivate restricted
comparisons or feature design without establishing an unbiased sample of all
materials.
